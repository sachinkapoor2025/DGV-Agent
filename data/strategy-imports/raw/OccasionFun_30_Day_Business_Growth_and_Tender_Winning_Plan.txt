Primary outcome: turn OccasionFun from a website and capability catalogue into a focused, active
business-development operation—creating conversations in Week 1, qualified opportunities and proposals
in Weeks 2–3, and a repeatable pipeline by Day 30.
Prepared for internal team execution. This report excludes internal financial/runway information.
The 30-day strategy is deliberately split into two lanes: near-term commercial projects (small fixed-scope work,
subcontracting, support and discovery) and long-term procurement opportunities (tenders, frameworks, primes and
supplier onboarding). A tender award inside 30 days is not controllable; pipeline creation and first paid work are the
controllable goals.

1. Executive strategy
Core positioning: OccasionFun is the UK-facing technology partner that engages clients, clarifies
requirements, manages the relationship and coordinates delivery—with DGV providing specialist
engineering delivery where disclosed, approved and contractually permitted.
Do not sell eight broad service lines equally. Lead with a small number of credible, buyer-friendly offers that solve
urgent problems and are easy to scope, price and start.

2. Website and positioning audit
The site is materially better than a generic startup landing page: it explains services, delivery lifecycle, the UK/DGV
relationship and procurement posture. The opportunity is to turn that credibility into conversion and proof.
Strengths to preserve
 Clear UK-facing company identity and company number; the site explains OccasionFun’s role in engagement,
 requirements, bidding and coordination.
 Service coverage maps to real demand: AWS/DevOps, software/API, AI, QA, web, data, secure engineering and
 managed support.
 Delivery model is described transparently: OccasionFun coordinates the client-facing engagement; DGV is the
 technology delivery partner.
 Public-sector page appropriately avoids blanket claims of certification or framework status and states that tender
 eligibility is assessed requirement-by-requirement.
 The site describes a delivery lifecycle, acceptance criteria, testing and handover—useful procurement language.

7. Outreach system and templates
Use personalised outreach with a concrete capability and an easy next step. The message should not read like a
generic offshore staffing pitch. Lead with the buyer’s delivery problem and the low-risk way to test the partnership.
Template A — Digital agency / consultancy
Subject: Delivery support for upcoming software / cloud projects
Hi [Name],
I’m reaching out from OccasionFun, a UK technology services company. We support client engagement and project
coordination, with specialist engineering delivery through DGV where appropriate. Our capabilities include
software/API development, AWS/DevOps, QA automation and data engineering.
If your team has overflow work, a delivery gap or a project needing specialist capacity, we can start with a clearly
scoped sprint or assessment rather than a long commitment. Would a 20-minute conversation next week be useful to
understand where we may fit?
Regards,
[Name]
Template B — Prime contractor / supplier partnership
Subject: UK technology delivery partner — cloud, software, QA and data
Hello [Name],
OccasionFun Ltd is a UK technology services company coordinating client engagement and project delivery with DGV
as a specialist technology delivery partner. We are interested in supporting prime contractors and consultancies where
additional engineering capability is required.
Our areas include AWS/DevOps, application/API development, QA automation, data engineering and defined
application support. We can provide a capability statement and relevant evidence, and we are transparent about
delivery roles, locations and subcontracting arrangements.
Could you advise the correct supplier or delivery-partner onboarding route and whether any current programmes
require these capabilities?
Regards,
[Name]
Template C — SME / CTO
Subject: A short technical assessment for [specific issue]
Hi [Name],
We help UK organisations address practical technology delivery problems across cloud, software, integrations, data
and testing. Rather than propose a large project upfront, we can scope a short assessment around [specific problem
observed] and provide a prioritised plan, risks and recommended next steps.
If useful, I can share a brief outline for a [cloud health check/API integration/QA automation] assessment. Would you
be open to a short call?
Regards,
[Name]

8. Bid library and procurement readiness
Build one controlled folder and one reusable response library. Every document must be current, approved and factually
supported. Government guidance highlights supplier registration, evidence, team continuity, subcontractor
transparency, tailored responses and a clear bid-management process.
Documents to prepare in Week 1
 Company profile and capability statement (2 pages).
 
 Company registration, ownership, supplier details and authorised contacts.
 
 Insurance schedule and proof of cover, if held; verify requested levels before bidding.
 
 Approved policies: information security, data protection, health & safety, equality, environmental, business continuity,
 complaints, modern slavery, anti-bribery, AI use—only publish or claim what exists and is approved.
 Team CVs/role profiles, skills matrix, availability process and continuity/cover model.
 
 3–6 project case studies with problem, work delivered, architecture/tools, role, timeline and outcome. Distinguish client
 work, internal projects, prototypes and anonymised work.
 Delivery methodology: discovery, scope, planning, development, testing, deployment, acceptance and handover.
 
 Quality assurance and change control templates.
 
 Risk register, assumptions, dependencies and mobilisation plan.
 
 Subcontractor/DGV information: legal relationship, role, delivery location, access controls, disclosure and client
 approval process.
 Commercial template: rate card or pricing model, fixed-scope offer descriptions, payment milestones and
 change-control wording.

Final recommendation
OccasionFun should spend the next 30 days acting like a focused UK business-development team,
not a passive tender website. Start selling short, low-risk technical engagements and delivery
partnerships immediately. Use tenders to build longer-term opportunity, but pursue only those where
eligibility, evidence, delivery and commercial fit are strong. The combination creates the fastest path to
conversations and a sustainable pipeline.
