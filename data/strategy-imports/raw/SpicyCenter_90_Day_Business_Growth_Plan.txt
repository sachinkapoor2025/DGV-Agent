Executive recommendation
For the next 90 days, focus on one business model: buyer acquisition + sourcing + export coordination. Sell to
importers, ethnic grocery stores, restaurants, food manufacturers, distributors and private-label brands. Use supplier
partners and confirmed buyer orders to avoid speculative inventory.
Your priority is not to become a giant marketplace yet. Close a few profitable, repeatable orders and prove a sales
process. Build automation only where it helps that process.

3. Three revenue engines
A — B2B wholesale: Target importers, distributors, grocers and food-service buyers. Quote after confirming destination,
quantity, grade, packing and shipping assumptions. Focus on repeat orders.
B — Sourcing desk: Buyer sends a product list or requirement. Source from verified suppliers, compare landed-cost
options, coordinate samples and export. Monetize through agreed margin, sourcing fee or commission.
C — Private label & starter assortment: Offer a small branded range to shops and online sellers through partners. Buy
packaging or stock only after deposit or confirmed order.

6. 90-day execution plan
Days 1–7: Stabilize and prepare
 Create a one-page company profile: registrations, sourcing, categories, process, contact and honest capabilities.
 
 Build quote and supplier-comparison sheets: product/spec, supplier, cost, pack, lead time, docs, freight, margin,
 Incoterm, quote validity.
 Focus on 20–30 products; confirm availability, minimums and realistic margins.
 
 Use a free spreadsheet CRM: lead, country, buyer, segment, products, quantity, status, next action, owner, date and
 value.
 Set up WhatsApp Business profile, catalogue, quick replies, email signature and sample workflow.
 
 Verify analytics and conversion tracking: enquiry, WhatsApp, email, quote and sample request.
 
 Response SLA: reply within 4 business hours; quote or clarify within 24 hours.
 
Days 8–30: Revenue sprint
 Build 300–500 targeted prospects across UK, UAE and Canada. Avoid mass spam; personalize.
 
 Daily target: 30 new messages/emails + 10 follow-ups + 5 buyer conversations, where lawful and appropriate.
 
 Offer one CTA: quote, sample kit, starter assortment or sourcing list.
 
 Publish three buyer pages: UK wholesale, UAE/GCC sourcing, restaurant supply. Include offer, buyer types, MOQ,
 enquiry form and proof.
 Negotiate suppliers for sample support, small MOQ, direct fulfilment, consolidation or credit only after trust.
 
 Qualify each buyer: SKU, form, quantity, destination, packing, timeline and target price.
 
Days 31–60: Improve conversion
 Review outreach metrics weekly; keep messages/markets that generate replies.
 
 Follow quotes on day 2, 5 and 10. Record lost reason: price, MOQ, freight, timing or trust.
 
 Create truthful case studies with permission from first customers.
 
 Offer replenishment reminders and monthly planning to buyers.
 
 Create 10 short videos using real product, team, packing and sourcing footage; repurpose on LinkedIn, Instagram,
 Shorts and WhatsApp status.
 Add downloadable wholesale catalogue and sample-kit page.
 
Days 61–90: Scale evidence
 Concentrate on the top 1–2 markets, 10–20 converting products and one buyer segment.
 
 Introduce private-label pilot only after buyer interest and partner costs are confirmed.
 
 Build referrals from buyers and sourcing partners.
 
 Use AI for lead research, translation, personalized drafts, quote extraction and follow-up reminders—human-verify all
 details.
 Paid ads only after conversion and margin are proven; start tiny with a stop-loss.

14. Compliance & trust—do not shortcut
Cross-border food trade is regulated. Verify destination requirements for product, importer, labelling, packaging,
certificates, customs classification, duties, food safety and shipment documents with official sources, the importer or
qualified broker. Requirements differ by country and product.
 Verify Indian export registrations and relevant product/food requirements for your role and shipment.
 
 UK/EU requirements must be checked for each product and arrangement; UK company registration alone does not
 make every import compliant.
 Maintain supplier records, specifications, batch/lot traceability, invoice, packing list and shipping documents.
 
 Confirm ingredients and allergens for blends; check label language and claims.
 
 Never claim certificates, organic status, lab results, pesticide limits or grades without documentary evidence.
 
 Confirm Incoterms, importer of record, duties/taxes responsibility, payment terms, insurance and dispute handling in
 writing.
15. Do NOT do in the next 90 days
 Build a marketplace for every importable product.
 
 Spend ■50,000–■1 lakh on ads before conversion and margin are measured.
 
 Buy stock because a keyword or trend looks attractive.
 
 Create hundreds of thin AI SEO pages.
 
 Offer credit to new buyers without risk controls.
 
 Accept orders that lose money after freight, fees and handling.
 
 Promise delivery, quality specs or certificates before verification.
 
 Spend weeks building an AI app with no paying customer.

Final recommendation
SpicyCenter should become a focused B2B spice sourcing and export partner first. Your website and AI skills
are assets, but cash flow will come from buyer conversations, accurate quotes, supplier reliability, deposits and
repeat orders. In the next 90 days, every team member should be connected to one of those outcomes.
Immediate launch: 30-day sales sprint; build a 500-prospect list across UK/UAE/Canada; send 30 personalized messages
daily; respond within 4 business hours; close paid samples or deposits before committing cash.
